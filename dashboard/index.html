Method in brief

Layers. A fixed schedule foundation (164 flights, cleaned; flight B4 443 omitted with provenance preserved) is expanded into a seat universe of N = 39,610 occupiable seats, one serial per seat in departure order. A load factor LF thins seats to passengers (Equation (2)); at LF = 1 every seat is a passenger, the worst case used throughout this page unless the slider says otherwise.

Entry times. Each passenger receives an entry time drawn from one of 104 candidate distributions over the check-in window, four hours to one hour before departure: 13 Normals, a Uniform, 6 exponential-growth and 6 exponential-decay shapes on the 4-hour window, and 78 skew-normals (39 left, 39 right). All draws are seeded and moment-verified (104/104 PASS).

Waits. A FIFO engine computes waits exactly: service start si = max(ai, di−1), processed di = si + t, wait wi = si − ai; with c counters, di = max(ai, di−c) + t. Two service regimes are solved: 180 and 200 seconds per passenger.

Allocation. A counter clears N = target/t passengers per target window; the desks a group needs at minute m are D(m) = ⌈AW(m)/N⌉, where AW(m) counts arrivals in the trailing target window (Equation (16)). Pooling is solved per flight, per airline, and per GHA, and compared against the physical supply of 168 counters on 13 islands.

Equation (16)D(m) = ⌈ AW(m) / (target / t) ⌉

Honest limits

Load factor 1 is a deliberate worst case; desk eligibility by cabin class and loyalty is specified but not yet applied; counters are treated as freely reassignable across islands; service time is homogeneous. The allocation rule is sufficient and conservative: true minima sit at or below the peaks shown here.
